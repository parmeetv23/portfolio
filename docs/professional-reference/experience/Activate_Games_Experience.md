# Activate Games

## Provenance and Completeness

This record is based only on the employee's descriptions in conversation. ## Overview

Worked at Activate Games' Sunridge Way location in Calgary, Alberta, from August 2023 to September 2024. Started as a Game Facilitator, performed some **unofficial Team Lead/keyholder duties for an unspecified period before the formal promotion**, and was officially promoted to Team Lead in March 2024. Continued performing Game Facilitator duties after promotion.

The facility had 11 interactive game rooms and typically served approximately 200–300 guests per day (facility-wide estimate). The work combined reception, bookings, customer assistance, gameplay support, hands-on technical repairs, and facility operations. Later responsibilities included leading shifts, training employees, resolving customer escalations, cash reconciliation, inventory, and headquarters reporting.

## Responsibilities

### Game Facilitator — August 2023 to March 2024

#### Guest check-in and gameplay

- Verified reservations, assisted walk-ins, created guest accounts, processed waivers, signed in guests, and issued wristbands.
- Took bookings and answered questions by phone and in person. Customer-service email was generally handled elsewhere.
- Processed payments and explained game rules, mechanics, safety procedures, and participation requirements.
- Monitored gameplay; entered rooms to demonstrate or explain games and assist guests having difficulties.
- Assisted with birthday parties, schools, corporate events, and other large groups.
- Helped manage guest concerns, including interruptions caused by malfunctioning games.

#### Technical troubleshooting and repairs

- Troubleshot technical failures throughout **all 11 game rooms**, with repairs needed almost daily.
- Diagnosed unresponsive sensors, malfunctioning LEDs, broken buttons, wiring faults, computer and motherboard problems, display and projector faults, and software issues.
- Investigated hardware faults through inspection, testing, and component isolation; replaced motherboards and other parts to restore room operation.
- Performed hands-on repairs including soldering, reconnection, wire splicing, connector replacement, and frequent crimping.
- Worked on unusual faults involving laser/light-based sensors and sensitivity adjustments, projectors, screens, and the photo-room fog machine.
- Flashed SD cards, loaded or installed software on Raspberry Pis, and adjusted settings through a user interface. **Did not primarily write or modify application code.**
- Tested repairs and prioritized bringing heavily used game rooms back into service when guests were waiting or complaining about downtime.
- Mainly diagnosed and repaired problems independently. Occasionally sought short guidance from headquarters technical support; the noisy environment made lengthy calls impractical.
- Used drills, screwdrivers, crimpers, soldering equipment, and other ordinary hand tools.
- Demonstrated repair methods to colleagues and taught troubleshooting and crimping, often through on-the-job shadowing.
- Balanced near-daily equipment repairs with live customer requests and other shift responsibilities.
- Game-room technical failures were operationally significant because high-use rooms broke more often and caused complaints when unavailable.

For an unspecified period **before March 2024**, the employee unofficially performed some Team Lead responsibilities and held facility keys while the formal job title remained **Game Facilitator**. This early assumption of responsibility should be retained as role progression, **not represented as an earlier official promotion**.

**Detail still unconfirmed:** exact starting month and precisely which lead duties were performed before formal promotion. Do not automatically assign every later Team Lead duty to this earlier period.

### Team Lead — March 2024 to September 2024

#### Leadership and staff coordination

- Continued Game Facilitator work while supervising teams of approximately **2–6 employees per shift**.
- Served as highest-ranking person on-site and acted as manager on duty during some shifts.
- Assigned staff to stations, delegated work, arranged breaks, monitored performance, and provided feedback.
- Handled day-of attendance problems, contacted absent employees, and sought replacement coverage when necessary. Did not ordinarily build advance schedules.
- Made many day-to-day operational decisions without an on-site manager, while referring some matters to headquarters or other managers.
- Could direct immediate staffing arrangements, including asking employees to come in or leave when appropriate.

#### Training and development

- Independently fully trained **six employees** in facility responsibilities and judged readiness for independent work.
- Helped additional employees through informal instruction and shadowing.
- Taught front desk, booking, customer service, game assistance, operational tasks, troubleshooting, and repair methods.
- Trained two people who became Team Leads: one partially and one comprehensively from the beginning.
- Regularly let colleagues observe real repairs to develop hands-on fault-finding and crimping skills.

#### Opening, closing, and operational management

- Held responsibility for opening and closing procedures: site security, keys, powering equipment on/off, checking rooms, preparing registers, cleaning task/checklist coordination, and securing cash.
- Counted tills, reconciled cash against system totals, documented outcomes even when balances matched, and placed funds in a safe.
- Made occasional bank deposits; documented cash-payment information in daily financial records.
- Monitored and updated inventory quantities for merchandise, guest performance rewards, and cleaning supplies; communicated replenishment needs.
- Emailed daily summaries to headquarters covering operations, outstanding issues, customer complaints or compliments, notable events, and problems needing attention.
- Was not confirmed responsible for purchasing budgets, formal vendor procurement, payroll, staff hiring/termination, or regular advance scheduling.

#### Customer escalations

- Addressed dissatisfied customers and complaints about refunds, booking errors, delays, game failures, and general service issues.
- Had authority to independently issue refunds and provide compensation such as free-play or 50%-off coupons where appropriate; exact authorization limits are unknown.
- Enforced facility safety and participation rules and intervened in confrontational situations involving other employees.

### Group visits

- Assisted birthday groups, school groups, corporate events, and other organized bookings, sometimes involving **up to approximately 100 attendees at once**.
- Major bookings were generally initiated through headquarters; sometimes acted as intermediary with organizers and helped with booking/invoice communication and administrative arrangements.
- Mainly communicated with organizers by telephone when they called or directly at arrival rather than routinely arranging events in advance by email.
- Counted arriving participants and helped teachers, volunteers, and organizers—many unfamiliar with the facility—coordinate check-in and move groups into game rooms.
- Large school visits were common around the end of the school year and other seasonal periods.
- Exact personal responsibility for generating or sending invoices is not confirmed. Do not claim end-to-end ownership of corporate sales or billing.

## Operational Examples

### Safety incident: water intrusion during severe weather

**Position:** Team Lead.

During severe Calgary hail and rain, water emerged from the washroom floor drainage area and spread sufficiently to create a concern around electrical equipment. This was an unusual incident at the site.

Actions personally taken:
- Shut down affected game rooms because of safety concerns.
- Moved guests away from affected areas and dealt with upset customers.
- Attempted to contact management; contacting Winnipeg-based management late in the day was difficult.
- Consulted an experienced manager from another facility to check that the chosen response was appropriate.

Cleaners handled the physical cleanup. The extent of any property damage, total downtime, and final outcome are not documented; no quantified claim about damage prevention is appropriate.

### Customer conflict: participation and supervision policy

**Position:** Team Lead.

Handled a confrontational customer interaction involving participation and supervision requirements. Explained and upheld facility rules, intervened when a colleague was approached aggressively, managed the escalation, and informed headquarters. The customer left and posted a negative review. This example supports independent judgment, safety-focused policy enforcement, and conflict handling; it does not establish a uniformly positive customer-service outcome.
